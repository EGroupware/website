When sustainable open source providers compete with price dumpers

General · February 13, 2025

Public sector procurement: when sustainable open source providers compete with price dumpers

Clear tender criteria for software projects

As described in a recent article on heise.de (in German), the Open Source Business Alliance (OSBA) has proposed an approach to prevent price dumping around free software in the public sector and to promote the selection of sustainable projects. It has now presented a catalogue of tender criteria.

Commitment to sustainable and secure models

EGroupware managing director Birgit Becker was actively involved in the working group. Together with Claus Wickinghoff – the two are the spokespersons of the OSBA procurement working group – she has been championing the topic for years. What looks cheap at first is often not economical in the long run:

“If the open source software used in public administration is to be maintained and developed further in several years' time, the actual manufacturer of the software, who takes care of it, must also share in the business. With proprietary software, this is always the case anyway: every licence sold also pays into the software manufacturer's coffers. In public tenders, the most economical offer has to be chosen. This is usually equated with the cheapest offer, which opens the door wide to price dumpers. If these providers only factor in their own services and treat the software as free of charge because it can be downloaded freely from the internet, then in the medium term nobody will find it economically worth­while any more to invest in the security and maintenance of the software being used. In the end, that comes back to haunt the public administration, which wants to use high-quality software. The cheapest offer then turns out not to have been the most economical one after all. Our criteria are meant to help public administrations decide which provider can implement a project sustainably, so that everyone in the open source ecosystem benefits and the software remains securely available in the long term.” Birgit Becker and Claus Wickinghoff, OSBA

Four criteria for the sustainable procurement of open source software

The challenge for public administrations is to define the right requirements and award criteria so that a contracting authority can reliably select the providers that offer secure, high-quality software and services.
In its position paper, the Open Source Business Alliance proposes four criteria:

  1. Relationship with the software manufacturer / community: Is there a business relationship between the provider and the software manufacturer or the relevant community, and to what extent do they support the contract?
  2. Ensuring that adaptations and patches are published upstream: How does the provider make sure that changes to the software are made available upstream to the general public again?
  3. Ensuring high-quality third-level support: To what extent is the contractor able to guarantee high-quality third-level support? Does it have the necessary expertise with the source code of the specific product, or can it guarantee the manufacturer's support?
  4. Securing the supply chain by supporting base components: Open source software often consists of many different components. Does the provider support the developers and projects of the individual software components built into its product? This also becomes relevant in connection with the Cyber Resilience Act under the heading “security in the supply chain”.

The full position paper is available for download as a PDF (in German).
